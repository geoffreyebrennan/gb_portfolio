Case studies

01 — Roles and permissions overhaul

Role UX Team Lead → Product Manager
Company Curve Dental
Focus Access control & user management
Outcome 9-month path to GA

Curve Dental's existing roles-and-permissions system was a genuine business liability. It was rigid, difficult to use, hard to administer, and not flexible enough to support a wide range of practice structures. This created real problems for sales, customer success and internal security: deals stalled in demos, practices struggled to manage access correctly, and the system could not meaningfully separate duties in a way that protected the business from fraud and operational error.

As UX team lead, I began by digging into the underlying customer behaviour rather than jumping to a design answer. I conducted interviews and design validation work with practice administrators, owner-operators and stakeholders across engineering, sales and customer success. I also worked closely with architecture and engineering teams to understand what the backend could support and where constraints would shape the solution. That early alignment was critical because the problem was larger than interface design — it required a better understanding of how customers actually structured permissions and how the platform needed to scale.

The result was a more granular, centralised administration model built to support both small offices and multi-site DSOs. It included much stronger role definitions, better visibility into who had access and why, location-aware controls, and a user directory designed around real administrative needs rather than assumptions. I then carried the project through to product management, helping sequence rollout, align stakeholders and guide implementation toward general availability.

This work showed me something important: some of the highest-leverage product work is not flashy, but it is foundational. When access control and admin workflows are badly designed, they create friction throughout the business and limit revenue. By reworking the system properly, we removed a meaningful bottleneck in the product and improved the trust customers placed in the platform.

02 — Perio charting rebuild

Role UX Team Lead / Lead Designer
Company Curve Dental
Focus Clinical workflow redesign
Outcome 10× usage increase

Periodontal charting is a clinical workflow that is both extremely common and extremely demanding. Hygienists gather a large amount of data quickly, often under time pressure, and the workflow has to support multiple individual preferences without compromising speed or accuracy. The existing module in Curve Dental was built in Flash and had become technically obsolete. On top of that, it was confusing enough that users often reverted to paper records instead of entering data directly into the system.

The challenge was not simply to recreate an old feature in a new stack. The real task was to rebuild a workflow that actually matched how clinicians work. I led this effort as design lead, working directly with dental professionals to understand the variation in exam practices, the sequence of charting, and the type of data that mattered most. That process surfaced a critical insight: there was no single correct pathway for periodontal exams. Hygienists disagreed on start conditions, order of measurement and which metrics mattered most, and the product had to accommodate those differences rather than forcing a single workflow onto the entire user base.

I designed a solution that paired anatomical accuracy with flexibility: a graphical mouth view for orientation, a tabular data-entry model for measurement, keyboard-first interactions for speed, and configurable exam behaviour that could adapt to different practice styles. We used SVG-based graphics and prototype testing to validate both the operational logic and the visual structure, then refined the product through alpha and beta feedback before general availability.

The result was a clear increase in product usage and a meaningful improvement in perceived quality. What had once been a feature users avoided became one of the product's defining strengths. That experience reinforced my belief that the strongest product decisions come from close observation of real users and a willingness to design for variability rather than the mythical average customer.

03 — Billing system update

Role Lead Designer / Product Manager
Company Curve Dental
Focus Billing workflow refinement
Outcome Faster, clearer and more scalable billing

Billing is one of those product areas that does not get the glamour of new features, but it absolutely determines whether a platform feels trustworthy to users. The billing module was being rebuilt to improve usability, performance and scalability while keeping the fundamental workflows familiar enough for customers to adopt without confusion. This is exactly the kind of project where design quality and product discipline matter: a small improvement in visibility or logic can reduce support issues, accelerate daily accounting tasks and cut accidental data-entry errors.

I was involved from concept through delivery, helping define the user experience and then carrying product responsibility through the implementation and release stages. The redesign focused on making repeated actions more visible, separating task flows more cleanly, and making the ledger easier to scan. It included improved filtering, smarter defaults, clearer invoice and payment flows, and stronger visibility into account status. These are not glamorous changes, but they are the sort of foundational updates that make complex software feel dependable.

What made the project successful was keeping the familiar shape of the workflow while creating clearer and more focused interactions. That balance matters: large improvements in backend capability and UI clarity should not come at the cost of forcing users to relearn the entire process. The end result was a billing experience that felt faster, more robust and better aligned with practice workflows, while also giving the product a stronger foundation for future feature work.

04 — Teaching AI to think like a CNC programmer

Role Senior Product Manager
Company CloudNC
Focus AI Manufacturing Software
Outcome Production AI product for CNC workflows

CloudNC is the clearest example of the work I do at the intersection of product strategy, technical depth and trust-building. The challenge was not simply to add AI to a workflow; it was to help CNC programmers adopt a system that could generate pragmatic machining strategies in a domain where the consequences of a bad decision are immediate and expensive. Every recommendation has to stand up to the realities of machine physics, tolerances and production constraints.

I work directly with machinists, engineers, customers and AI researchers to define what value the product can credibly generate and how that value should surface inside the real production workflow. That means focusing on the points where automation reduces repetitive cognitive load without asking users to surrender judgment. It also means building system confidence through transparency, clear constraints and iterative feedback loops.

The result is a production-grade AI product that helps users move faster without compromising technical credibility. It is a strong example of the product leadership mode I use across domains: understand the underlying constraint, validate customer pain, translate that into a defendable roadmap, and keep the technical depth close to the product decision.

Product leadership approach

The common thread across these projects is not just UX or strategy in isolation — it is disciplined product leadership. I care about roadmap sequencing, trust, rollout quality and measurable outcomes, not just the appearance of a feature.
